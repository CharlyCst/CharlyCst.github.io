A Pile of Papers

Last week was my last week at EPFL, and while packing I stumbled on the old pile of papers I was reading in the early days of my thesis. So, for the sake of nostalgia, here is a list of some of my favorites!

The Scalable Commutativity Rule

It’s quite rare to have the equivalent of physical laws in systems design, but here is one: if an interface is commutative, then it admits a conflict-free implementation, i.e. one that scales nicely to a large number of cores.

Memory Resource Management in VMware ESX Server

How can the host and guest kernels co-operate to manage memory? With a memory balloon! The host needs more memory? Inflate the balloon to reclaim pages from the guest. Such a simple and clever idea, I love it!

On Micro-Kernel Construction

Liedtke’s classic myth-busting paper on micro-kernels. To read with Gernot Heiser’s “From L3 to seL4” paper for an overview of the L4 kernel design space.

Formal Requirements for Virtualizable Architectures

Another law of systems design! An ISA is classically virtualizable if all sensitive instructions (that read or update privileged state) are privileged (they trap in user-mode). That paper lays out the theoretical foundations of virtualization.

Dune

Dataplanes greatly benefit from direct access to low-level hardware features such as page tables, TLBs, interrupts, and devices. Turns out with modern virtualization extensions we can just expose all of that to processes, safely! Dune is the modern version of the exokernel design.

The CHERI capability model

CHERI is an ISA extension that adds hardware support for capabilities (base + bounds) on pointers. Turns out there are tons of fun research to do with new ISAs! New kernel designs, new isolation and memory management tricks, or smart pre-fetching!

Multiprogramming a 64kB Computer Safely and Efficiently

The TockOS paper! Rust really opened a whole new design space for kernels and operating systems, and Tock is one of the first systems to really make use of it. One of my favorite OSes and a glimpse into the future!

Scaling Symbolic Evaluation with Serval

Serval is one of those papers that demonstrate that symbolic execution is an excellent verification tool for some classes of programs, those with bounded execution traces. Turns out there are a lot of such programs hiding within system software!

And there are many more that I omitted, or that I liked but didn’t print!
